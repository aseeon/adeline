Agent activity panel: four proposals

Each mockup uses the same session: two turns in adeline, the second still running. Everything is clickable. Hover a row for more, click it to expand. The panel header matches the chat header. The activity list runs from the top, and the stats sit in an island at the bottom, the same height as the composer and aligned with it. Click Show in chat or scroll the chat up, and the panel stops following until you jump back to the latest message. The chat is static. Nothing streams.

What we can show

Stats in the mockups are limited to what ACP gives us today, what we already store, and what we can derive from our own transcript. Anything estimated is marked with ≈.

A. Ledger

A flat list with a fixed time lane on the right, and six numbers in the island below. Turns are thin headings. Hovering opens a card over the chat, next to the row. Clicking expands the row in place.

  • Good: closest to today's panel and the cheapest to build. Every row is one line, so a long session stays easy to scan.
  • Cost: six numbers are always visible, even when you only care about one. You can't see where the time went without reading the lane.

B. Trajectory

The closest to Harness. The island holds a strip with three lanes: you, the agent and tools, laid out on a compressed time axis. Clicking a tick jumps to its row. Rows start with a short kind tag and end with the result. Hovering a row swaps the island for that row's details, so nothing covers the chat.

  • Good: you can see the shape of the whole session at a glance: long commands, idle gaps, failures. The peek never covers the chat.
  • Cost: while you hover the list, the stats are hidden. The strip gets crowded after roughly 60 tools and would need to zoom or bucket.

C. Waterfall

Built around time. The island gives active time and a bar splitting it into commands, model and edits. Each row has a small waterfall bar placed within its turn, like a browser network panel. The hover card shows when the step started and how long it took.

  • Good: answers "why was that turn slow" without opening anything. Here, a release build took 40% of the turn.
  • Cost: the bars are tiny at 302px. Most rows are short reads, so many bars end up as 2px slivers.

D. Outline

The least detail up front. The island shows three numbers and what's running now. All stats opens the rest above it. Each turn is one row headed by your prompt. Finished turns collapse to a summary line, and the running turn stays open. Consecutive reads and searches group into one "Read" row, and edits group into "Edited". Running commands always get their own row.

  • Good: by far the most compact. A 40-turn session is 40 rows until you open one, and the prompts work as the table of contents.
  • Cost: it takes more clicks to reach a single tool. Groups hide the order of steps inside them.
