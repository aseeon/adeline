Many open projects in the title bar

Every proposal keeps the window controls fixed at the right edge, and the active project and the Projects menu always visible. The windows are 1200px with 12 open projects and 800px with the same 12, like the screenshot.

A. Fold, then an overflow menu

This builds on today's behaviour. Names fold to letter marks first. When even the marks don't fit, the tabs that don't fit go into a "+N" menu at the end of the strip, VS Code style. The active tab is never moved into the menu. If a hidden project needs attention, a dot appears on the "+N" button.

B. Shared width, then scroll arrows

Browser style. All tabs shrink evenly, and names truncate down to the mark plus a few letters. Below that, the strip scrolls. Arrows at both ends show there's more, and edge fades show which way. The arrows mark hidden projects that need attention with an accent colour. Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+1–9 still reach every project.

C. Busy projects stay, quiet ones stack

When space runs out, the strip shows the active project and any project with something waiting, with full names. Quiet projects collapse into a stack of marks at the end of the strip. Clicking the stack opens the Projects menu, already filtered to open projects. Your attention goes where the work is.

D. Projects move to a vertical rail

Projects leave the title bar and become a column of large marks on the far left, like Slack workspaces or Discord servers. Badges show attention. The title bar keeps one breadcrumb for the active project and has plenty of drag area. A column holds about 15 projects at 800px height, then scrolls. Optionally this switches on only when the tabs overflow, or as a setting.

Four more

These take different angles from A to D: give the bar more height, let you choose what stays, swap the strip for a switcher, or group the projects.

E. A second row of tabs

When one row is full, the title bar grows a second row that spans the whole width, under the window controls. Names stay readable. If two rows still aren't enough, the second row folds to marks. The bar never grows past two rows, and it goes back to one as soon as one row fits.

F. Pinned marks, then recent names

Browser-style pinned tabs. Projects you pin sit at the left as square marks that never fold or move, and a dot shows attention. After a divider come the most recently used unpinned projects, with names, as many as fit. Older ones live in the Projects menu. You choose what stays, so the bar is predictable.

G. A switcher instead of a strip

No tab strip at all. The title bar shows the active project as a switcher button, plus marks for the last three projects you used. Ctrl+Tab, or clicking the switcher, opens a grid of every open project with its attention and last activity, like Alt+Tab. Hold Ctrl and keep pressing Tab to move through it, or type to filter.

H. Project groups that collapse

Like Chrome's tab groups. You put projects into named, coloured groups. The group with the active project stays open. When space runs out, the other groups collapse to a chip with their name and count, plus a dot if a project inside needs attention. Clicking a chip opens that group in place.

Four more again

These move projects out of the title bar, make the marks the main way to switch, or show less detail for projects you haven't used in a while.

I. Project tabs in the status bar

The bottom bar is mostly empty, so the project tabs move there, like a taskbar or tmux's window list. The title bar keeps only the active project and the current mode as a breadcrumb, so it never overflows and stays draggable. The bottom strip folds and scrolls the same way the title bar does today.

J. A dock of marks

Every project is always a mark. Only the active one shows its name, in a pill. Hovering a mark shows its name in a tooltip, and a dot shows attention. Marks are about 30px, so 12 projects fit at 800px and around 25 at 1200px before the strip starts to scroll. It works like a dock: you learn it by colour and position.

K. Less detail for older projects

Tabs are sorted by most recent use, and the detail each tab shows depends on how recently you used it. Recent projects keep full names. Older ones become marks. The oldest become small coloured squares at the end, which open the Projects menu. A project that needs attention moves up to at least a mark. Nothing disappears until there are 30 or more projects.

L. Projects in the side panel

Projects become the top level of the side panel, with each project's chats nested under it. The title bar shows a breadcrumb, so it never overflows. Switching projects and switching chats happen in one place, and the panel scrolls like any list. Other modes, such as Docs or Workflows, nest their items under the projects in the same way.
